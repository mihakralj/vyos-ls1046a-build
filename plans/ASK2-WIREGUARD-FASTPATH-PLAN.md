**2026-10-15, updated 2026-11-07 · dpaa1 · Status: open.** This is the single active WireGuard plan. The completed work, which covers the measurements, profiles, the P0/P1/P2a/P2b-1 builds and the gate results, has moved to the archive:

**Pending decision (proposed 2026-11-08, not yet confirmed by the owner).** WGFP's whole QMan footprint is sink `qman_create_fq`/`qman_init_fq` once at first bind (a timeout marks the pool failed and keeps the queues), one ERN tag FQ and one no-confirm TX FQ per egress port, and `qman_query_fq_np` from the root-only stats file; it never shuts down, retires or destroys an FQ, and P3 reuses the same sinks with CPU decryption (no CAAM). The five QMan shutdown counterexamples are therefore unreachable from WireGuard and the MC cases reduce to stale diagnostics. Proposal: treat the QMan portal protocol as platform hardening that does not gate P3, move the WGFP-relevant parts of FE teardown ownership (withdrawal under traffic, ingress/egress netdev removal, disengage with live flows) into stage 3's acceptance tests, and start stage 3.

**Next tasks, in order.** (1) Stage 3 P3: per-flow ingress provenance in the sink frame (ingress ID) and outer RX records accepted only from the expected port; withdrawal under traffic with no stale sink flow; family policy for the outer RX record (`dpaa_wgfp_family_ok()` on the port the outer flow arrives on); RX distribution across CPUs (one outer flow saturates one CPU today); gate G3 down ≥ 2.6 Gbit/s on four flows, kill below 2.2, plus replay-window and out-of-order netns tests. (2) Stage 2 leftovers: first-port SYNC counter, F-269 timeout recovery. (3) Stage 5.

### Path to full acceleration (2026-11-07)

Today only IPv4 TX is accelerated: about 2.8 to 3.1 Gbit/s on four flows, against about 1.7 to 1.9 Gbit/s in software. Decrypt, IPv6 and single-flow scaling stay on the software path, and encryption always runs on the CPUs because CAAM Era 8 has no ChaCha20-Poly1305. Reaching bidirectional, dual-family acceleration takes the stages below, in dependency order. Stages 1 and 2 are not WireGuard features, but P3 cannot be re-enabled without them; stages 6 and 7 are demand-driven.

2. **Close the safety gates.** These block every later stage:
   - DDR-walker quiescence: a discriminating test that holds or identifies a specific in-flight ehash lookup across a SYNC, plus proof that port disable (F-274/F-211) is a real barrier. The 2026-11-05 load-correlation run is not that test. **F-307 walker probe (2026-11-07, working tree, host-tested):** FMan 210.10.1 writes `packet_count` at record+256 on every HIT of a 320-byte record whatever STATS_EN says (T-M8-3, 2026-08-22), and the lookup copies the record with one `dma.read256` before comparing the key. With the root-only countdown `fsl_dpaa_fman.ehash_walker_probe=N`, each of the next N successful deletes reads that counter right after the unlink (c0) and after the SYNC (c1), then retains the record and the replaced cumulative nodes instead of freeing them; nothing is written to the record or to a register. `ehash_walker_probe_inflight` sums c1 − c0, the HITs by lookups that already held the old head while the SYNC ran: this is the positive control the earlier run lacked. `ehash_walker_probe_late` sums each retained record's current count minus c1 on every read; any nonzero value means a lookup reached an unlinked record after its SYNC completed, so INV0 is not a reclamation barrier and P3 stays blocked. Retained blocks are never freed (a busy `dma_pool_destroy()` leaks its pages), up to 4096 records, so reboot after an experiment. `bin/test-ehash-walker-probe.py` runs the generated helpers and the real `del_key()` against mocks (probe off, retention, in-flight and late accounting, SYNC failure, full array) under ASan/UBSan, and two mutants are caught; the fixup applies idempotently and `fman_pcd.o` compiles with `W=1` in the CI-order replay tree. Board procedure, on a cold boot: one ntuple rule on eth3 for a fixed UDP 5-tuple that dell1 sends at the highest single-flow rate the rig allows, `echo 1000 > /sys/module/fsl_dpaa_fman/parameters/ehash_walker_probe`, then 1000 delete and re-add cycles of that rule with the flow running; read `kept`, `inflight` and `late`, and `late` again after 60 s. Verdict: `inflight > 0` and `late = 0` is the discriminating pass; `inflight = 0` is inconclusive (raise the packet rate); any `late > 0` fails the gate. This does not cover port-disable quiescence or the F-269 timeout path. **Board result (2026-11-07, `.185`, image `2026.11.07-2039-rolling`, operator power-cycle, about 380 s up at the start): PASS.** pktgen on dell1 sent `10.99.1.112:40001 → 10.99.1.185:51999` at 1 Mpps (64 B) at an ntuple enqueue record (`action 0`); 1000 delete and re-add cycles took 5.0 s with no failures. `kept` = 1000, `inflight` = 465 and `late` = 0, still 0 after 60 s idle and after a further 60 s of 1 Mpps traffic at the re-added record. No BUG, stall or new QMan error with `softlockup_panic` and `panic_on_rcu_stall` armed, and the BMan pools stayed stable. A completed INV0 SYNC therefore acts as the reclamation barrier for single-record ehash lookups on 210.10.1. **Chained bucket (2026-11-08, image `2026.11.08-0228-rolling`, power-cycled): PASS.** Per-port tables use mask `0x7FFF`, shift 0, so the bucket is `(crc64_raw(key50) >> 48) & 0x7FFF`; UDP `10.99.1.112:{40001,44516,52296,65005} → 10.99.1.185:51999` all land in `0x507f`, which `fe_flow` confirmed on silicon (`tbl[3] flow[0..3] bucket=0x507f`). With the other three as static records, 1000 delete and re-add cycles of the 40001 record under 1 Mpps gave `kept` 1000, `inflight` 733, `late` 0, still 0 after 60 s more traffic; the chain stayed intact, with no stall or QMan error. The barrier therefore also holds across cumulative-node chain rewrites. **Reclamation audit (CI-order tree, 2026-11-08):** every ehash record and cumulative-node free sits behind a completed INV0 SYNC or frees memory the hardware never saw: `add_key` frees replaced nodes after its SYNC (quarantine on failure), `del_key` frees the record and replaced nodes after its SYNC (quarantine and poison on failure), `flow_drain` clears all heads, SYNCs, then frees, `recover` frees quarantined generations after a later SYNC, and the publish/add failure paths free unpublished allocations. Port disable is not an ehash reclamation barrier, so its quiescence matters only to the non-ehash users (AF_XDP detach, the unreachable F-211 IPv6 arm path, the disabled F-291 recover) and does not gate WGFP. **This gate item is closed** except the F-269 natural-timeout path, whose quarantine-and-recover code is host-modelled but has never fired on silicon. A drop record (`action -1`) could not be used: every HIT of the F-247/F-248 EXIT-FE terminal raises QMan `ErrInt: Invalid Enqueue State` with `ecir.fqid 0x0` from DCP 0 at any rate (no buffer leak). WGFP uses enqueue records only, so this does not block WireGuard, but it blocks shipping drop offload.
   - F-269 timeout quarantine and recovery on a natural pending SYNC, not only an injected one.
   - QMan portal protocol: the strict host model still reports 2 MC and 5 shutdown cases. This needs full-transaction command ownership, identity-aware FQRN/FQRL handling, MR/DQRR/SDQCR ownership during shutdown, and owner retention on busy or timeout results.
   - FE teardown ownership: an end-to-end contract for exported disengage, bridge/FDB, egress FQ and netdevice lifetime, PPP and VLAN withdrawal, and stale external DESTROY.
   - ~~The F-168/F-215 RX SYNC fold correction~~ shipped in `cd124b9b` (image 0333 onward). The `fman_pcd_port_recover()` pool-offset error is contained, not fixed: F-291 refuses `fe_recover` and `fe_disengage_full` with `-EOPNOTSUPP`, which is enough for WireGuard; the offset fix is needed only before recovery is re-enabled. The first-port SYNC counter still needs serialized per-port transition accounting and checked timeout handling.

**Stage 3 implementation (2026-11-08, working tree; host-tested, board gates pending).** The sink queues are now per ingress port (`struct wgfp_port`: four workers on CPUs 0..3, created on the port's first record), and `struct dpaa_wgfp_frame` carries `ingress_ifindex`. Both the writer and the sink key a flow on tuple plus port, so equal tuples on two ports select their own flow instead of being ambiguous; the global `create n` sinks remain only for the old lab tests. Admission comes from `wg_receive()`: before `wg_packet_receive()` it records the exact outer tuple, `skb->skb_iif` and the wg device in a per-CPU observation ring. That hook runs after IP input and the INPUT hook, so an observation proves the tuple was delivered to the WireGuard socket; `wgfp_pst_rx_sync()` admits a record only for the peer's current endpoint observed this way, installs it on the **observed** ingress port (which may differ from the transmit route in an asymmetric setup), and re-checks the port's `offload` family every refresh. The old conntrack/keypair fallback is gone. `wgfp_rx` is a writable module parameter again (default off). One outer flow no longer pins one CPU: with `fanout <batch>` set, its record points at a per-port distributor FQ which re-enqueues the untouched buffer to that port's workers, switching worker every `batch` frames so a burst stays together and an inner flow is reordered only at batch boundaries. It uses a dedicated ERN tag queue and `rx_enq_fail`/`rx_ern` counters. `echo "fanout <batch>" > /sys/kernel/debug/dpaa_wgfp/ctl` applies to new records (toggle `wgfp_rx` 0→1 to reinstall existing ones); the default is 0, the record targeting one worker, because the board measurement below found fan-out slower. This is software counter-based distribution after a single exact-match record, so it needs no KeyGen or pool-channel change and keeps one NAPI per portal. Family removal, endpoint change, `wgfp_rx=0`, peer death and unbind all withdraw the RX record; frames already in a sink after the delete find no flow and are released (the deliberate unmatched-frame containment), so no stale record or peer is used; only frames in flight at withdrawal can be lost. Host tests: `tests/wireguard_fastpath_rx.py`, `tests/wgfp_sink_lifetime.py`, `tests/wgfp_flow_identity.py` updated and the WGFP host suite (85 tests) passes; `bin/testrig-wgfp-p3.sh` runs the admission, distributor-spread and withdrawal-under-traffic gates and prints download retransmits (reorder evidence; compare `fanout 0` and `fanout 32`), and G3 is measured with `echo 1 > .../wgfp_rx; WGFP=1 REPS=3 PORT=eth4 DIRS=down CELLS=wg-v4 bin/wg-line.sh`. `dpaa_wgfp.o` and `fastpath.o` compile warning-free with `W=1` against the 6.18.60 tree; patch 4018's new `socket.c` hunk applies to the pre-4018 tree. Not built into an image yet; the board still holds the F-307 walker probe's retained records (`kept` 1000), so power-cycle before the first P3 experiment. Board order: cold boot; tunnel; `bin/testrig-wgfp-p3.sh`; then `wg-line` down with `wgfp_rx=1` at `fanout 32` and `fanout 0`, plus `wgfp_rx=0` as the software baseline; then `bin/testrig-wgfp-p2b3.sh` with `wgfp_rx=1` (roam changes the outer tuple, so the record must move) and the upstream `netns.sh`.

- **Why fan-out loses.** With fanout 32 the eth3 transmit sinks sealed 3.43 M frames against 0.72 M with fanout 0, about 4.8×, for less download. Those are the return ACKs: batch-boundary reordering makes the receivers ACK every segment instead of delaying, and sealing the extra ACKs costs more than the spread saves. The box was 69% busy, so fan-out also adds a serial delay of its own (distributor hop on CPU 0). Software counter distribution without order restoration is therefore not the lever; a reorder-free spread needs QMan order restoration (ORP) or must happen after WireGuard's in-order stage.
- **Where the limit is now.** With fanout 0 no core is saturated (busiest 85%) yet the rate stops at 1.93, so the cap is a serial stage, most likely the peer's single `wg_packet_rx_poll` NAPI (in-order dequeue, replay check, GRO, then IP forwarding and eth3 transmit, all on one CPU) or the sink CPU's copy and UDP checksum. Next: `perf record -a -g` on a fanout-0 download to name it before any further change.

4b. **P6, zero-skb decrypt (next, decided 2026-11-09).** Upload already passes 4 Gbit/s (4 tunnels 4.21) because sealing never builds an skb: the sink seals in place in the BMan buffer and `dpaa_wgfp_xmit()` enqueues it to the egress no-confirm TX FQ. Download still runs nearly the whole Linux path even with P3: skb copy, decrypt workers, the per-peer serial `wg_packet_rx_poll()`, GRO, `ip_forward`, qdisc, `dpaa_start_xmit` (1T 1.96, 4T 2.29 Gbit/s on image 2054; crypto is not the limit, the P2a lab seal reached 465k pps / 5.3 Gbit/s). P6 mirrors the TX fast path on receive. The P3 sink, for a type-4 data message of a peer with a current receiving keypair and a learned inner flow:
   1. `dma_sync` the frame and decrypt in place (`chacha20poly1305_decrypt`, the counter as nonce), never copying to an skb;
   2. validate the counter against the **same** replay window upstream uses (`keypair->receiving_counter`, under its lock, the logic of `counter_validate()` in `receive.c`), so packets that punt to the stack and packets decrypted here share one window; update the peer's rx timers and stats the way `wg_packet_rx_poll()` does, rate-limited like the TX path's 10 ms timer block;
   3. check the inner source against the peer's AllowedIPs (`wg_allowedips_lookup_src()`);
   4. rewrite in place: drop the outer Ethernet/IP/UDP/WG headers, write the inner flow's learned Ethernet header (egress MAC pair, optional VLAN), decrement TTL / hop limit and fix the IPv4 checksum, then `dpaa_wgfp_xmit()` to the egress port's no-confirm TX FQ.
   
   Inner-flow learning: a decrypted inner flow is admitted only after the stack has **forwarded** it (observed in the forward path after the firewall, the same rule as P3's stack-observed admission), so P6 never forwards what the firewall would drop; the egress device, next-hop MAC and MTU come from `ip_route_output` + neighbour lookup, refreshed once a second and RCU-swapped like the TX templates; the egress port must be an ASK2-engaged DPAA port with that family enabled. Anything else punts to the existing P3 path (skb, upstream receive): handshake, cookie and keepalive messages, decrypt or replay failure, an unknown inner flow, an oversize or non-IP inner packet, a frame that needs a second buffer, a TTL of 1. Ordering: one outer flow stays on one sink (`fanout 0`), so packets of an inner flow leave in order; the punt path can reorder only around a learning or withdrawal edge.
   
   Withdrawal: route, neighbour or MTU change, family-policy change, peer removal, rekey (records follow the current keypair), `wgfp_rx=0` and unbind withdraw the inner records, as P2b-3 does for TX. Telemetry: `rx_fast`, `rx_fast_punt` by reason, `rx_fast_replay`, `rx_fast_aip`.
   
   Gates: G6 download, 4 tunnels at least 4.0 Gbit/s and 1 tunnel at least 3.0, IPv4 and IPv6 inside, DUT CPU below the P3 figure; correctness, all with P6 on: the P2b-3 suite (rekey, rekey at 120 s, roam, AllowedIPs, PMTU, wg0 delete), upstream `netns.sh`, a replay/duplicate-injection test (a replayed captured packet must be dropped by the shared window), an out-of-order test (packets reordered within the window are accepted once), and a firewall test (a forward drop rule added mid-flow must stop the flow within one refresh). Risk: the replay window and nonce handling now run in our code beside upstream's; keep them on upstream's own state and functions, never a copy. Effort 1-2 weeks to a gate-quality prototype. Board teardown and lifecycle gates are the same as T1-T4 in `ASK2-MASTER-PLAN.md` §4.9.

The UDP-checksum and buffer-lifetime fixes shown in the P3 receive path shipped in image 0629. The design rules still apply:

- The per-peer fast-path state lives inside the `wireguard` module; `dpaa_wgfp` supplies the sink FQs and record operations. The relevant ingress port must already have an armed PCD table.
- Records are installed by the slow path; a MISS uses the normal stack. Failed record deletion and admission provenance still need the handling described below.
- Distinct TX flows are assigned round-robin to shared sink FQs, preserving each flow's sink order; a peer's outer RX flow currently uses one sink.
- TX seals in place in the BMan buffer; P3 RX currently copies to an skb.
- Known TX declines and invalid P3 headers are punted; an unparseable or unmatched sink frame is dropped.

## 3. Remaining phases, gates and kill criteria

AGENTS.md S0 applies to every phase that touches FMan: run at least three qdrant queries, cross-check against `arch/fman-microcode-210-programming-reference.md` and `specs/fman-keygen-flow-key-spec.md`, read back every silicon write, and cold boot before each experiment. Arm `softlockup_panic` and `panic_on_rcu_stall`, and never experiment on `eth0`.

### P2b-2. Automatic flow install

On the first `wg_xmit` for an inner flow whose peer, endpoint and keypair are valid and whose ingress port is ASK-engaged, the module asks `ask.ko` to insert the record. The record is removed on flow teardown, peer removal or disengage. This needs the registration API in section 4.

**Gate:** no debugfs steering. Four inner TCP flows reach at least 2.6 Gbit/s `up`, and `pcd-snapshot diff` is clean after disengage.

### P2b-3. Slow-path punt and AllowedIPs

The worker must hand these cases back to the stack by building an skb and calling the `wg_xmit` slow path, then withdraw the record:

- AllowedIPs mismatch;
- keypair near `REKEY_AFTER_*`;
- no neighbour;
- oversize frame;
- keepalive due;
- peer removed.

**Gate:** the upstream `tools/testing/selftests/wireguard/netns.sh` passes, endpoint roaming and a forced rekey keep the tunnel up, and there is no Invalid MAC on stock dell2.

### P2b-3 implementation (2026-11-02, commit `1d6f64bd`, board-tested on `.185`)

All of it is in `fastpath.c`; no DPAA or `ask.ko` change.

- **Punt (`wgfp_punt()`):** a frame the seal path declines is copied into a fresh skb and received again with `netif_receive_skb()` on the ingress port of its learned flow (`fl->ifindex`). The stack then forwards it (the software flowtable or the classic path) into `wg_xmit()`, or elsewhere if routing changed, with TTL, ICMP, PMTU, firewall, AllowedIPs and handshake queueing intact. The DPAA buffer goes back to its pool. Punts are the exception (handshake gaps, roaming, oversize), so the copy is acceptable. A frame of no known flow (manual debugfs records) is still dropped. The `wgfp_peer` read-out adds `punted` and `aip_withdraws`.
- **Which declines withdraw the record:** oversize (`ip_len > wg0 MTU`) and a dead peer punt and withdraw. No keypair, an expired keypair, `REJECT_AFTER_MESSAGES`, no template, TTL ≤ 1 and a roamed endpoint only punt. Rekey at `REKEY_AFTER_*` was already triggered from the seal path (P2b-1), and keepalives stay with the stack's timers, which the seal path feeds.
- **Roaming:** each frame compares the template's outer destination with `peer->endpoint.addr4` unlocked (the same unlocked test `wg_socket_set_peer_endpoint()` uses). A mismatch punts and runs the template refresh at once (`mod_delayed_work`). A refresh that finds no IPv4 endpoint, no route via the egress port or no valid neighbour now clears the template, so frames punt instead of leaving with stale headers.
- **AllowedIPs:** the 2 s aging pass looks up each installed record's inner destination with `wg_allowedips_lookup_dst()` (an skb holding one IPv4 header as the probe) and withdraws records that no longer map to the bound peer.
- **Re-learning without a ct:** `wgfp_ct_admitted()` takes the conntrack proof from the skb when present, else looks the entry up by tuple (`nf_conntrack_find_get`, default zone) and requires `IPS_SEEN_REPLY`, no NAT, no master, no helper. This fixes two board-observed gaps. A UDP flow was never learned, because the flowtable takes it on the first reply, so every later packet from the plain side arrives without a ct (`[HW_OFFLOAD]` in conntrack, `flows=0` after 1500 packets). And a withdrawn flow could never come back.
- **wg0 deletion:** a netdevice notifier schedules a stop on `NETDEV_UNREGISTER` of a bound WireGuard device, so `ip link del wg0` no longer waits on the references peer mode holds. The notifier does not cover physical egress removal.

### Closing step A implementation (2026-11-02; deployed on 1818 and 2011)

**Multi-peer and multi-device (P2c).** `wgfp_peer` takes a list of wg devices (`echo "wg0 wg1" > /sys/module/wireguard/parameters/wgfp_peer`; `off` unbinds). Writing a list rebinds the whole set (full teardown and rebuild; flows relearn within a second). Every peer of a bound device is eligible. Per-peer state (`struct wgfp_pst`: peer reference, template, timer pacing) is created by the peer's first learned flow and freed by the aging pass once it has none. The sink path finds the flow lock-free (RCU hash; entries unhashed with `hash_del_rcu()` and freed with `kfree_rcu()`) and the peer through it, so the four sinks stay shared and no per-peer FQs are needed. The egress port now comes from each peer's own route: `dpaa_wgfp_egress(dev)` returns a handle to a no-confirm TX FQ allocated once per DPAA port (each `dpaa_alloc_offload_tx_fq()` call creates a new FQ, so it must not be called per refresh), and `dpaa_wgfp_xmit()` takes it. A peer whose route leaves through a non-DPAA port gets no template and stays on the stack. The read-out adds `peers` and `dev`. Up to 8 devices.

**Use-after-free fixed.** The aging pass dropped the lock to read the silicon counters and then wrote to `job[i].fl`. A FIN or a withdraw in that window let the flow worker free the entry. It now re-finds the flow by tuple and only touches it if the table still holds that same entry.

**Configuration (patch `data/vyos-1x-059-wireguard-offload-auto.patch`, 2026-11-07).** WireGuard offload is implicit: if the port under the tunnel is offloaded, the tunnel is offloaded, with no WireGuard setting (the same rule as VLAN, NAT, PPPoE and bridge in AGENTS.md S3). The earlier `set interfaces wireguard wgN offload ipv4` leaf was removed before it reached `main`, so no migration is needed. `interfaces_wireguard.py` computes the full list of non-disabled wg interfaces on every commit, caps it at the kernel's `WGFP_MAX_BIND` (8) with a warning naming the interfaces left in software, and writes it to `wgfp_peer` only when it differs from the bound set (`dev=` in the read-out), because a write rebinds. A kernel without the parameter is silent. Flows are installed only for traffic arriving on a port with `set interfaces ethernet ethN offload ipv4`; everything else stays on the software path.

**CLI control safety (2026-11-07, host-tested).** Before changing an interface, patch 059 now rejects unreadable, malformed or `stopping=1` WGFP status. An active reservation blocks an offload-related change even when the current device names match, while an inactive reservation does not block software-only configuration. Binding errors are configuration errors instead of successful commits, and status is reread after `ENODEV` so a retained failed teardown cannot be mistaken for a missing interface. If an opted-in interface has not yet been created, binding is deferred without replacing the existing set; a later interface apply can retry it. If deferral would leave a no-longer-requested binding active, the change is rejected before the interface update instead; creating the new interface and replacing the binding requires separate commits. Deferral and an unsupported kernel still only warn, so the requested offload can remain unapplied if no later apply succeeds. The checks are not atomic with binding or netdevice removal, and an error after `wg.update()` does not roll back earlier runtime changes. These changes are not in image 0333 and do not close the lifecycle gates.

FQ-local tail drop is the mechanism F-199 proved works for FE-fed FQs; the CGR path does not see FE enqueues. Harness: `bin/testrig-wgfp-soak.sh overload` (gate: side-flow loss ≤ 1%, balanced eth3 pool) and `bin/testrig-wgfp-soak.sh soak` (10 min, 4 flows; gate: balanced eth3/eth4 pools, `xmit_fail` 0, no kernel errors, `fe_flow` empty, `pcd-snapshot diff` clean).

**Sink-depth gate suspended (2026-11-03).** `dpaa_wgfp` prints `qm_mcr_np_get(&np, frm_cnt)`, which masks the raw management-response word without byte-order conversion. If the count is big-endian, as other portal fields are, this little-endian host can report zero for a nonempty queue. The soak runner now refuses to run unless the kernel advertises `frm_cnt_encoding=host`; no current image or staged kernel code advertises it. Add that marker only after correcting and validating the count against a known queued frame, then rerun the gate.

The pending sink diagnostics retain the legacy `frm_cnt` display but mark its encoding `unknown` and expose the unmasked `frm_cnt_raw` and `byte_cnt_raw` words. A failed QMan query is reported as `query_err` instead of silently omitting the result. These raw words make a future controlled byte-order check possible; they do not prove queue emptiness. The soak gate still refuses them and also rejects missing, duplicate or contradictory encoding markers.

**Sink-count byte order verified on hardware (2026-11-04).** On `.185`, image `2026.11.04-0252-rolling`, a 20-second WGFP four-stream run (2.6 Gbit/s, 50 ms sampling) produced 313 non-zero sink samples out of 5172. In every one the masked `frm_cnt` read 0, while after a 32-bit byte swap `byte_cnt / frm_cnt` was exactly 1434 bytes in all samples inspected (frame counts 2 to 41). The QMan queue-status counters are therefore big-endian on this host, and the earlier zero sink depths did not show empty queues. The working tree now prints host-order `frm_cnt` and `byte_cnt` decoded with `be32_to_cpu()`, keeps the raw words, and advertises `frm_cnt_encoding=host`; the soak gate also requires `byte_cnt=0`. This is pending a build; no soak or lifecycle test has run with it, and the teardown, FE-deletion and ehash-quiescence gates below remain open.

**Sink retirement hazard, source review (2026-11-03).** In image 0629, `wgfp_sink_free()` calls `qman_oos_fq()` immediately after `qman_retire_fq()`, including when retirement returns `1` for asynchronous completion, and continues to `qman_destroy_fq()` after errors. In the available 6.18.60 QMan source, OOS requires a retired, empty FQ with outstanding ORL notifications consumed; destroying an unquiesced FQ can return without removing its callback-table entry. The shipped code nevertheless disables and deletes NAPI, clears `created`, and resets `wgfp_nsinks`, allowing a later create to reuse the sink object. The debugfs `destroy` command also does not reject an active consumer. This is a source-derived risk, not an observed board failure; the exact deployed QMan tree has not been independently checked.

Do not use the sink `destroy` command for experiments until producers and FE records can be quiesced, asynchronous retirement and FQRN completion handled, queued FDs drained and released correctly, and OOS/destroy failures propagated without reusing live objects. Unbinding alone does not establish those conditions while failed FE deletions and sink-depth decoding remain unresolved.

**QMan retirement contract (source review, 2026-11-04).** The API contract carried by `kernel/common/patches/board/4019-qman-enqueue-fqid.patch` is strict: `qman_retire_fq()` returns `0` for immediate completion, `+1` when retirement is asynchronous, and a negative error otherwise. In the asynchronous case, FQRN completion may reach the callback before `qman_retire_fq()` returns, so callback state must be initialized before the call and the FQ object must remain alive through completion. `qman_oos_fq()` requires a retired, empty FQ and all ORL notifications consumed. A sink teardown must also release any FDs delivered during drain and keep NAPI/callback ownership until QMan can no longer invoke them. The existing host lifetime test confirms that `destroy` remains refused and that uncertain sink creation retains its FQ/NAPI objects; it does not implement or validate retirement.

The local, uncommitted containment rejects debugfs `destroy` with `-EOPNOTSUPP`. If sink creation fails, including an ambiguous QMan initialization timeout, it retains every allocated FQ and its NAPI and refuses a new pool until reboot; `pool_failed=1` reports this state. The legacy P1/P2a `RECREATE=1` and P2b manual mode also refuse to destroy sinks, including on older images. This trades recoverability for object lifetime safety; it is not a drain implementation and does not make unbinding or stale FE records safe. Host mocks and an ARM64 object build cover the guard, but it is not in image 0629 and has not been tested on hardware.

The soak gate also compares both synchronous `xmit_fail` and asynchronous `xmit_ern` against their starting counters and fails if either rises or is unavailable. Its process cleanup checks PID start time and command identity through a pidfd, and it refuses to unbind a different WGFP device. The device check and sysfs write are not atomic, so they do not prevent every concurrent binding race.

**QMan TX ownership correction (implemented 2026-11-02, shipped in image 0629).** The original 6.18.60 `qman_enqueue()` returns zero even if `qm_eqcr_start_stash()` or `qm_eqcr_start_no_stash()` returns NULL, contrary to its documented full-ring error contract. Read-only disassembly of image 2011 confirms the NULL branch reaches a zero return; ring saturation was not induced or observed. Consequently `dpaa_wgfp_xmit()` could count an unsubmitted FD as successful, skip its retries and strand its BMan buffer. Patch `4019` supplies a separate `qman_enqueue_fqid()` that returns `-EBUSY` on a full ring, so WGFP's existing eight retries and final release execute.

The existing WGFP egress object also contains only an FQID, so its QMan software-ERN tag is zero rather than a registered callback. On this image, a rejection with that tag would encounter the F-102 NULL guard; source and live disassembly show that guard loops without consuming the MR entry. No such rejection was reproduced. Patch `4019` also permits a destination FQID to use a separately registered callback tag. `dpaa_wgfp` reserves one tag FQID for its built-in lifetime, counts asynchronous ERNs in `xmit_ern` and `xmit_fail`, and returns their BMan buffers; the tag is independent of the port-owned egress FQs. `xmit_ok` counts EQCR submissions, not confirmed wire delivery, and an asynchronous rejection can increment both `xmit_ok` and `xmit_fail`. The F-102 NULL-MR handling for other callers remains a separate unresolved defect.

The new function deliberately duplicates the portal enqueue sequence rather than changing the legacy API: a global `-EBUSY` change makes unhandled failure paths reachable in other clients. In particular CAAM QI does not unmap its request descriptor on synchronous enqueue failure, and the DPAA AF_XDP path releases and reserves completions for an entire descriptor batch before enqueueing, then abandons the remainder on error. Completions are ordered, so immediately completing the tail could release earlier in-flight buffers. These are source-derived concerns, not reproduced failures; the generic `qman_enqueue()` defect remains open pending a cross-client ownership fix. Patch `4019` was generated from an isolated clean canonical preimage, but its canonical-branch integration and round-trip remain pending; do not run the round-trip script against the dirty shared kernel checkout, because it performs a checkout. Its `verify` command can also return success with missing or new patches, and can print `VERIFIED` despite missing patches; inspect the full result and confirm `4019` is included rather than relying on its exit status.

The `sealed`, `xmit_ok`, `rx` and `rx_stack` hot telemetry counters are also per-CPU locally, summed across all possible CPUs on read; the cryptographic nonce remains the shared atomic counter. Sharding the RX counters can avoid contention between peer sinks, but cannot distribute a single tunnel across sinks. On an idle `.185`, a synthetic four-thread atomic microbenchmark measured roughly 34 ns per operation for one shared cache line versus 3.7 ns for separate per-thread cache lines. That does not measure the kernel change or prove a throughput gain. The scoped QMan path and counters have host regressions and ARM64 object builds, but need a new operator-installed image, controlled TX/RX A/B, and safe full-ring/ERN tests before production claims. Physical-port unregister and ifindex reuse remain unsupported: the cached egress FQIDs belong to the port, and tag lifetime alone does not keep those FQs or ingress buffer pools alive.

**DMA contract gap, source-derived.** Mainline `dpaa_eth.c` maps pool pages `DMA_FROM_DEVICE`, but WGFP modifies those pages on the CPU and `dpaa_wgfp_xmit()` syncs the transmit range as `DMA_TO_DEVICE`. Linux's DMA API requires a bidirectional mapping when the CPU writes a device-receive buffer; the fast path also treats a DMA address as a physical address and reuses it across ingress and egress devices. These assumptions need an ownership and mapping audit, including bounce buffers and device domains, before non-coherent or translated DMA can be supported. On the tested `.185`, the FMan device-tree node is `dma-coherent`, there are no per-port non-coherent overrides or IOMMU groups, and the SWIOTLB `used` and high-water counters were both zero after the smoke. This reduces concern for that run but does not establish a general DMA contract or prove a hardware fault; no DMA mapping change was made.

**Security review (A4), `fastpath.c` against upstream `send.c`/`noise.c`.**

- Key material: peer mode never logs or exposes keys. The P2a lab key is written through a root-only (0600) parameter and is not readable back.
- Keypair and counter handling match `wg_packet_encrypt`/`encrypt_packet`. The seal path checks `sending.is_valid` and `REJECT_AFTER_TIME`, takes the nonce with `atomic64_inc_return() - 1`, rejects `>= REJECT_AFTER_MESSAGES`, and never reuses a consumed nonce (a declined frame burns its counter like an upstream drop). Keypairs are freed through `call_rcu()`, so the sink's RCU read section covers the use without a keypair reference.
- Padding is the same rule as `calculate_skb_padding()` (16-byte multiple, capped at the MTU, and `ip_len <= MTU` is checked first).
- Buffer bounds are checked before every write: headroom `>= WGFP_ADDED`, `off + 14 + plain + 16 <= buf_size` (3584), so the outer `tot_len` cannot overflow.
- Rekey and timers run as upstream: the timers are fed every 10 ms per peer, and a handshake is triggered on `REKEY_AFTER_MESSAGES` or `REKEY_AFTER_TIME` as the initiator. Handshake and cookie messages never use the fast path.
- Semantic deltas, all shared with nf flowtable offload and documented: after admission a flow skips the firewall and conntrack TCP window checks until its record is withdrawn; an AllowedIPs change takes effect within one aging period (2 s); a non-first IP fragment of an offloaded flow is not matched (no ports) and takes the stack. A first fragment is sealed as a normal packet, which WireGuard permits.
- Carry cost: every upstream WireGuard security fix in `send.c`, `noise.c`, `timers.c` or `allowedips.c` must be checked against `fastpath.c`.

### P3 implementation (2026-11-02; deployed on 1818 and 2011)

**Measurement first.** Software `down` on `.185` (image `2026.11.02-1632-rolling`, 4 TCP flows, `wgfp_peer off`): 1.61 Gbit/s. CPU2, the RX CPU of the single outer UDP flow, sits at 100% softirq, while the other three are 84–88% busy with the decrypt/encrypt workers. `perf` on CPU2: `__netif_receive_skb` 82%, of which netfilter `nf_hook_slow` 40% (`nft_do_chain` 12.6%, `nf_conntrack_in` 8%), IP input and routing ~10%, UDP socket delivery ~5%, `wg_receive`/`wg_packet_receive` 16%, DPAA RX 15%. The serial cost is the outer packet's trip through IP input and the INPUT firewall, not decryption, which upstream already spreads over all cores.

**Silicon check.** The plan assumed an ehash record could steer the inbound outer flow, but qdrant (2026-09-06) held that to-self traffic bypasses the ehash (`pr_word & 0x0044`). Re-tested: a record for UDP `10.99.2.113:40001 -> 10.99.2.185:51999` (the DUT's own eth4 address) steered 200 of 200 packets to a sink (`tbl[2]` = eth4). To-self UDP does hit records; qdrant corrected.

**Design.** One ehash record per peer for its inbound outer flow (endpoint -> local source address, UDP, endpoint port -> listen port, on the port the peer's route uses), kept in step with the template by the refresh work (`wgfp_pst_rx_sync()`). Image 2011 installs it based on the conntrack or untracked-keypair checks described below, and withdraws it on a template or endpoint change, peer removal, unbind or `wgfp_rx=0`. It does not age (it lives with the peer state). The sink copies the frame into an skb and calls upstream `wg_packet_receive()`; the version shipped in image 0629 validates the IPv4 header and the IPv4/IPv6 UDP length and checksum before doing so, and punts invalid packets to `netif_receive_skb()`. Decryption, the replay window, the inner AllowedIPs source check, ordering, handshakes and cookies stay upstream code; IP input, netfilter, conntrack and the UDP socket lookup are skipped. Read-out adds `rx` and `rx_stack`. Peer state is created by TX learning, so a receive-only peer gets no RX record; one outer flow is still one sink and one CPU.

**Gate G3** unchanged: `down` >= 2.6 Gbit/s on four flows, kill below 2.2; plus the P2b-3 suite with `wgfp_rx=1`.

**Untracked outer flows and admission gap.** VyOS untracks the WireGuard UDP ports (`system conntrack ignore`, applied automatically by vyos-1x patch 057 and by `bin/wg-matrix.sh`). Image 2011 admits an untracked tuple when the peer has a current receiving keypair; image 1818 lacked that path, so its `down`/`bidir` cells used software RX. A current keypair proves an earlier handshake, not that a newly configured exact endpoint and ingress passed INPUT. Likewise `IPS_SEEN_REPLY` alone is not proof that the specific inbound packet passed INPUT: conntrack runs before the filter. These are source-derived concerns, not board reproductions; P3 is not production-hardened until exact-tuple, ingress-specific admission is proven and tested, including manual endpoint changes and roaming. Existing NAT/helper checks do not close this gap.

**Family gap.** With C, a bound tunnel also accelerates inner IPv6 flows. `dpaa_wgfp_flow_add()` checks that the ingress FMan port has a routed ehash table, but does not check the per-port `offload ipv4|ipv6` mask that `ask_hw_flow_family_ok()` applies to normal ASK flows. The WGFP parser and record builder accept both families, including P3's outer tuple, so an IPv4-only Ethernet engagement does not itself enforce IPv4-only admission. Since the port's family leaves are now the only policy, this check is required before IPv6 learning is enabled (stage 4). This is a source-derived gap, not a measured board result.

The fix must enforce the WireGuard interface's selected **inner** family and the ingress Ethernet port's selected family without silently removing IPv6 support from explicit experiments. P3 classifies an **outer** UDP tuple before decrypting, so its family policy must distinguish outer and inner addresses rather than assume they match. Family changes also need to prevent racing installs and withdraw existing disallowed records safely; the unresolved hardware-delete failures must not be hidden. Test IPv4-only, IPv6-only and dual-family ingress, mixed inner/outer families, and policy changes with active flows before claiming the CLI gate.

**Flow identity and deletion gaps.** The writer distinguishes tuple plus ingress ifindex, but `wgfp_flow_lookup()` at the sink compares only the tuple, and the frame does not carry an ingress identity. Equal tuples on two ports can select the wrong flow or peer. Do not use BPID as the identity: ports can share pools. The normal worker quarantines failed withdrawals as `WGFP_STUCK`; the teardown path is now being hardened to retain that owner rather than drop it. The distinct sink identity ambiguity remains open. These findings are source-derived and have not been reproduced on the board.

**Delete-result and owner contract (in progress).** The F-269 working-tree transform returns a failed delete SYNC to its caller, quarantines the removed flow and replaced cumulative nodes, poisons that table, and rejects mutations until a later SYNC succeeds. The opt-in F-285 barrier also preserves HC errors such as `-EIO` and `-ENODEV`, not only `-ETIMEDOUT`. F-219 duplicate eviction stops before reinsertion when deletion returns an error. A failure before the delete reaches FMan, including loss of the ingress netdevice, can still leave an entry linked. External ASK/TC callers must retain their software owner on that outcome; unregistering the sink consumer while a stale entry can steer packets to it causes the no-consumer fallback to release rather than forward those packets.

The `.70` vendor's `mono-1.2.1` source distinguishes three outcomes: deleted and synchronized; unlinked but awaiting a barrier; and not provably unlinked. It quarantines the first kind of uncertainty without repeating the destructive unlink, and retains possibly linked entries until ports are quiesced and the entry can be resolved. This is a useful ownership model, not a drop-in implementation for ASK2. ASK2 needs an explicit result and retained owner for each state, admission/reinsert exclusion while deletion is unresolved, and a barrier or proven port quiescence before reclamation. A PCD barrier alone does not drain already-enqueued sink frames; QMan completion and RCU/peer lifetime still need independent proof. Exercise each outcome and its races with fault injection in host tests before a controlled board test. The vendor and ASK2 findings are source-derived; no timeout or stale-record failure was induced on `.185`.

The contract must cover bucket generations, not just the record being deleted. After an insert-side SYNC timeout, a retained old cumulative node can still refer to other active records; those records cannot be reclaimed while an in-flight lookup may reach that generation. The working-tree transform now blocks mutations while poisoned, synchronizes clear/drain before reclaiming records and nodes, and moves per-port table teardown ahead of dependent MURAM frees. Its walker test models a completed SYNC as sufficient and assumes detached ports during drain; neither assumption proves the required silicon or caller behavior. Table/port teardown, parent-device release, and every external deletion caller still need a complete ownership contract and fault coverage.

**Withdraw-failure quarantine (F-273; built and soak-checked 2026-11-04).** `wgfp_flow_work_fn()` moves a flow to `WGFP_STUCK` after a failed hardware delete, so the worker and aging skip it and do not relearn/reinsert over a record that may still be linked. F-273 exports the F-271 `ehash_sync_wedged` counter; `dpaa_wgfp_flow_del()` compares it around deletion and reports `-ETIMEDOUT` if a SYNC wedge was observed. `withdraw_fail` counts quarantined flows. CI run 41776621065 built image `2026.11.04-0501`; `.185` passed a 120 s soak at 2.736 Gbit/s with all ehash SYNC counters at zero. Image `2026.11.04-0531` also passed a 120 s soak at 2.857 Gbit/s with no timeout or withdraw warnings. These are normal-operation checks, not fault injection. The three-outcome delete contract, bucket-generation ownership and port quiescence remain open.
**Failed-delete teardown containment (working tree, host fault-injected 2026-11-04).** `wgfp_flows_flush()` now returns the first withdrawal error, retains the failed flow as `WGFP_STUCK`, and never retries a potentially destructive unlink on a later stop attempt. `wgfp_peer_stop()` returns that error before clearing the binding, unregistering the DPAA consumer, dropping device/peer ownership, or releasing a module reference. Stop marks the binding and peer states as stopping, drains RCU learning readers, and cancels age/refresh/install work; `wgfp_flow_withdraw()` also refuses to queue new deletes once its peer is stopping, closing a sink-callback race with the flush. A failed teardown therefore disables new learning while leaving the consumer available to handle packets for any unresolved record. Peer-start pins the WireGuard module for the binding, so `rmmod` cannot unload the callback code while a failed teardown retains the consumer. Stop controls preserve their state and report the error; the peer status exposes `stopping=1`. `tests/wgfp_flow_teardown.py` fault-injects a failed delete and confirms flow retention, no duplicate delete on retry, and no new withdrawal work once stopping; `tests/wgfp_peer_lease.py` confirms the binding, consumer, device reference and module pin survive a failed flush, then release on a successful cleanup. The direct host C harnesses pass, and the changed fastpath object compiles against the generated Linux 6.18.60 tree. Existing rig cleanup already stops through `wgfp_peer_lease` and halts on a failed stop, so no rig changes are required for this containment gate. This is fail-closed containment, not a way to resolve an uncertain FMan record: persistent failure intentionally pins state until a future recovery contract or reboot. The hardware delete/quiescence and QMan drain gates remain open. Do not run hardware stop/unbind or matrix tests yet.

**WGFP/QMan fail-closed regression rerun (2026-11-05).** `pytest --noconftest -q tests/wgfp_flow_teardown.py tests/wgfp_peer_lease.py tests/wgfp_sink_lifetime.py tests/qman_wgfp_enqueue.py` passes 8/8. **FMan delete-caller audit and required contract (source-only, 2026-11-04).** The WGFP wrapper compares the global `ehash_sync_wedged` counter around `fman_pcd_fe_flow_del()`; a concurrent wedge can therefore conservatively quarantine an unrelated WGFP delete. Other callers are not uniformly safe on errors: `ask_flow_offload.c:ask_fe_flow_remove()` discards the result, the `0198` tc-flower destroy path removes/frees its software rule even when deletion fails, `dpaa_cls_flush()` in `0194` ignores each result, and F-219 ignores duplicate-eviction errors before inserting. The normal `0194` classifier delete preserves its rule on an error, but its flush path does not. More fundamentally, a timed-out F-269 insert can leave a cumulative-node generation referencing multiple active records; deleting/reinserting another record or draining the table can free memory still reachable from that generation. The old generation is not tracked, and clear/drain has no synchronized-reclamation or proven-quiescence gate.

The implementation must encode these outcomes in the FMan API rather than infer them from a global counter:

| Outcome | Hardware state | Required owner action |
|---|---|---|
| `DELETED_SYNCED` | The replacement head is published and the applicable barrier has completed. | Retire the removed flow and every replaced generation whose references are covered by that barrier. |
| `UNLINKED_UNSYNCED` | A head change was published, but one or more old generations may still be read by FMan. | Quarantine the removed record, every record reachable from those generations, and the generations themselves; block mutations that could invalidate that ownership map. |
| `STILL_LINKED` | The operation did not publish an unlink; the record may still be reachable from the current head. | Keep the software owner and consumer; do not report deletion or release associated state. |

This table is an ownership contract. The RM's general FPM SYNC ordering semantics provide a source-level basis for a successful `INV0` to drain earlier packet lookups, but a timeout does not establish that barrier. In particular, a later insert timeout poisons the bucket generation even if a subsequent per-key delete of another flow succeeds locally. The implementation must retain all objects whose reachability is uncertain and make clear-all, table destruction, and port teardown fail closed rather than treating a timed-out/zeroed head or disabled BMI/QMI as proof. QMan sink-FQ retirement and RCU/peer lifetime remain separate gates.

The later F-283/F-284 trials did not reproduce a real timeout.

1. **Add passive, default-off telemetry first.** F-283 measures each actual `fman_pcd_ehash_sync()` INV0 attempt without changing its timeout, retry count, register writes, or return behavior. `ehash_sync_diag_enable` defaults off; read-only parameters expose attempts, completed vs still-pending reads, max polls/ns, a coarse elapsed-time histogram, the last `FMFP_EXTC` value, and the last value observed with INV0 pending. A focused host C test confirmed telemetry does not alter INV0 writes or reads. On `.185` image `0553`, the enabled telemetry observed 80/80 INV0 completions during bounded eth3 ehash configuration churn, 0 pending, 0 timeouts, max 2.24 µs, max 2 polls. This was not packet traffic and is not a stale-lookup test.
2. **Measure normal silicon behavior, not a manufactured timeout.** On `.185`, cold-boot the diagnostic image, confirm `ehash_sync_inject=0`, capture an idle baseline, then generate bounded normal ehash add/delete activity under controlled traffic on an isolated test port. Record attempt counts, poll/elapsed distributions, timeout log and register value, traffic rates, MURAM budget, and kernel warnings. Stop on the first actual timeout, FPM stall, DMA error, MURAM drift, or management-path degradation. A timeout counts as silicon evidence only when the production 100 ms wait expires with INV0 still pending; F-282's post-completion injection is software-path evidence only.
3. **Do not shorten the timeout or re-arm a pending INV0 yet.** The current F-271 retry path writes INV0 again after an attempt times out. Qdrant/reference evidence gathered so far does not establish whether reasserting a still-pending INV0 is safe. Until that is resolved from authoritative register semantics or a controlled vendor implementation, a microsecond timeout is not an acceptable proxy for a natural hardware timeout, and no test should deliberately exercise the pending-reassert case.
4. **Treat walker quiescence as a separate proof.** A latency histogram or a successful `fman_port_disable()` proves no more than the observed register/poll result; neither alone demonstrates that an ehash lookup cannot access a retired DDR generation. Do not rewrite or free an ehash record to create a canary until the record's update atomicity and safe terminal action are verified against the 210.10.1 format. Any eventual canary test must retain all old records/generations, use a dedicated isolated port and valid sink FQ, tag ingress packets so delayed queue delivery is distinguishable from a post-barrier stale lookup, and include a positive control that demonstrably detects the stale access. First prove that the control itself cannot corrupt MURAM/DDR or affect eth0.
5. **Keep destructive tests last and separately approved.** No invalid bucket pointer, forced DMA fault, forced port-disable timeout, or production SYNC skip is part of the current experiment. Such a test requires a reviewed fail-safe test image, an isolated datapath, serial capture, cold-power recovery available, and a maintenance window. Until both the passive silicon measurement and a safe canary with positive control pass, F-274/F-211 walker quiescence and real silicon SYNC-timeout recovery remain open, and P3 stays blocked.
6. **A/B the vendor's candidate barrier: Host-Command SYNC (F-284 transport board-validated).** On `.185` image `0553`, cold-booted, `init` on O/H hwport 0x02 / QMan channel 0x809 succeeded; `sync 10000` confirmed all 10,000 frames (14.5 µs min, 95 µs average, 161 µs max). During the same bounded eth3 configuration churn as the INV0 measurement, `sync 100000` confirmed all frames (93 µs average, 116 µs max), no errors or stray confirmations; the ethtool operations all succeeded, MURAM stayed at 58,764 bytes, and eth0/eth3/eth4 stayed up. `fini → init → sync 100 → fini` also succeeded. This corrects the former “no HC” conclusion and validates transport/completion on our stack. ### Closing step C implementation: IPv6 (2026-11-02; in image 2011, IPv6 gates pending)

**C1, IPv6 inner flows.** The flow tuple carries a family and 16-byte addresses (`struct dpaa_wgfp_tuple`, compared and hashed whole, always built zero-initialised). `wgfp_key()` emits the silicon-verified dual-lane layout for IPv6 (family 0x60, zero 8-byte v4 lane, src6 @9, dst6 @25, then proto/ports/VID/SID as for IPv4; qdrant 2026-08-21 and 2026-09-06), in the same 50-byte per-port table, so no FMan change is needed. One parser (`wgfp_parse()`) serves the sink path and learning: plain IPv4 (no options, no fragments) or IPv6 without extension headers, TCP or UDP; learning reads the headers through `skb_header_pointer()`. The seal path decrements the hop limit for IPv6 (no checksum). The conntrack lookup and the AllowedIPs probe handle both families.

**C2, IPv6 outer (endpoint).** The template is built per family (`wgfp_tmpl4()`/`wgfp_tmpl6()`): IPv6 uses `ip6_route_output()`, `ipv6_dev_get_saddr()` when the endpoint has no source, the ND table and `ip6_dst_hoplimit()`. Both builders now pass `wg->fwmark`, as upstream `send4()`/`send6()` do (the IPv4 lookup previously ignored it). The outer header length is per template (Ethernet + 20 or 40 + 8); the headroom test and the transmit offset follow it. The IPv6 UDP checksum is mandatory, so it is summed in software over the datagram after encryption (`csum_partial` + `csum_ipv6_magic`, about 3% of the per-packet cost). The endpoint compare checks the address family first (an IPv4 compare against an IPv6 endpoint would read `sin6_flowinfo`). The P3 RX record and `wgfp_rx_frame()` handle an IPv6 outer flow (no extension headers; anything else goes to the stack). Unknown: whether the DPAA RX headroom (`f->off`) is at least 64 bytes; if not, every IPv6-outer frame declines and is punted (correct, but no fast path). The `punted` counter will show it.

**Harness.** `FAM=6 AUTO=1 bin/testrig-wgfp-p2b.sh` drives inner IPv6 flows; the IPv6 outer case is `OUTER=6 KEEP=1 bin/testrig-wireguard.sh setup wg-v4`. The P2b harness now picks a fresh source port per run (`CPORT0`).

**Atomic WGFP reservation (2026-11-04).** Commit `e14e871e` adds the root-only `wgfp_peer_lease` parameter in `fastpath.c`. `reserve <token>` refuses an existing peer or lab consumer, and blocks legacy peer writes and lab starts under the same mutex. `bind <token> <altnames>` resolves only the lease's `wgo-<token>-...` device altnames and rejects duplicate devices; `stop <token>` unbinds but retains the reservation, and `release <token>` requires an inactive binding. Binding registers the notifier before publishing the device set, then checks each device's registration, namespace and resolved name under RTNL. If a bound device unregisters or moves to another network namespace, the notifier marks the binding and schedules the entire leased binding to stop; the reservation remains held. This is deferred teardown, not synchronous datapath quiescence. The notifier records the event instead of relying only on `reg_state`, which remains `NETREG_REGISTERED` during a namespace move; stale work from an old binding cannot invalidate a new one. Module unload unregisters the notifier before cancelling pending stop work. On ASK2, both software-only and `WGFP=1` rigs reserve before creating resources, hold the reservation through link deletion and release it last; the single-tunnel capability probe has its own reservation. Software runs never bind or stop WGFP and refuse cleanup if their reservation has become active. The journal reconciles lost reserve, bind, stop and release responses without adopting another owner's binding. Vendor runs do not require this API. Both ASK2 modes refuse older kernels without it, including image 0629, rather than falling back to a racy check. An older software lease with resource markers but no reservation journal is retained for manual inspection, not automatically deleted. The legacy control interface remains available when no reservation is held, but its namespace-move and unload handling also changed.

The token coordinates privileged control-plane writers; it is not an authorization boundary. **Crypto queue replacement contract.** In 6.18.60, `wg_queue_enqueue_per_device_and_peer()` puts a packet in the peer's ordered queue as `UNCRYPTED` before publishing it to the shared device crypto ring. If that ring is full, the `-EPIPE` path marks the already-queued packet `DEAD` so it cannot block later packets. Per-CPU decrypt workers drain the shared ring; release/acquire packet-state publication lets the peer's serial NAPI poll consume completed packets in order, validate replay counters, and release peer and keypair references. A replacement must retain those ordering, overflow, publication and lifetime guarantees; comparing CPU samples alone is not a contention measurement. Measure queue occupancy, lock contention and wakeups under the target workload before choosing a replacement.

**Requested optimization areas.** TX already spreads distinct inner flows over four sinks; it does not parallelize one inner flow. P3 skips the outer software stack but still copies into an skb, relies on upstream decrypt workers, and needs TX learning to create peer state. Counter-based RX distribution is not implemented: adding the changing WireGuard counter to the shared ehash key would break exact-tuple lookup, so a separate silicon-proven dispatch mechanism is required. The current kernel already uses `ptr_ring` and per-CPU crypto workers; replacing those queues requires a measured bottleneck and must retain replay, ordering, backpressure and teardown semantics. Offline-port reinjection is not implemented: the Qdrant phase 1h record reports that patch 0191's scheme setup still produced no capture; the earlier default/error-FQ test also saw no frames. Whether the input FQ drained is unverified because its count may be byte-swapped. The 0191 report lacks an exact image or raw log, and current-stack behavior remains unverified. Authentication, AllowedIPs, firewall/conntrack admission, NAT, routing, TTL, and buffer ownership must remain correct before any decrypted packet bypasses the stack.

The SDK's KeyGen `extractedOrs` can affect FQID bits without adding the extracted byte to the comparison key. It is an investigation candidate, not an implementation: ASK2's FE `ENQUEUE_PKT` uses a fixed record FQID, so whether a counter-derived FQID can survive that terminal is unproven. A historical non-FE exact-match CC graft using an FQID-override exit stalled the RX FIFO; bypassing the FE terminal is not a safe shortcut (`arch/fman-fe-ehash.md` §1). Header-relative extraction, short or fragmented packets, queue allocation, ordering and reversible teardown also need proof. The former live oracle at `.106` is unavailable; `.70` now serves as its vendor-ASK replacement, but hardware programming still requires the mandatory live-oracle and SDK cross-check. Do not program KeyGen or FE on this hypothesis.

**Pool-channel alternative, source review (2026-11-03).** Mainline `dpaa_eth.c` allocates an RX pool channel and adds it to each online affine portal's static-dequeue mask for default ingress queues; its PCD queues are instead assigned to dedicated portal channels in bands. `HOLDACTIVE` is set on ingress FQs and `AVOIDBLOCK` on TX confirmation FQs. WGFP currently creates one FQ per CPU on its dedicated channel, without either flag. NXP's QMan documentation permits multiple portals to service a pool channel, but does not establish that one WGFP FQ will distribute packets concurrently or preserve the ordering this path needs; pool scheduling is not counter-based steering.

Simply moving a WGFP sink to that pool is unsafe without a portal-aware redesign. A sink has one `napi` and one `portal` pointer: `wgfp_dqrr()` masks the reporting portal's interrupt, overwrites that pointer and schedules the sink's NAPI, and `wgfp_poll()` polls and re-enables whichever portal the pointer then names. Callbacks on different portals could overwrite this state or leave an interrupt masked; the sink's ordinary counters also assume its dedicated consumer. In addition, `qman_p_static_dequeue_add()` silently masks out pools a portal cannot access, and the current public QMan API exposes no matching static-dequeue removal function. A new pool needs checked portal access, per-portal NAPI/IRQ ownership, a reversible subscription and allocation lifecycle, and a measured ordering/replay test before any hardware trial. These are source-derived risks, not observed failures. The `.70` vendor's `cdx` and `ask_flowtable` modules are loaded, but no equivalent WireGuard pool-channel mechanism was established there.

Leftovers after those gates, in order of value:

- **Receive-only peers get no RX record.** Peer state (and with it the P3 outer record) is created by TX learning, so a peer that only sends to us (a one-way UDP download, a peer whose inner flows enter on a non-DPAA port) stays on the software receive path. Design bounded peer-state activation with authenticated, current-tuple firewall provenance, peer removal, reaping and re-engagement; a current keypair alone does not establish that a manually changed endpoint passed INPUT.
- **Zero-copy RX and punt** (master plan §4.8 item 3): every P3 frame is copied into a fresh skb today.
- **Per-flow cap** (P5, master §4.8 item 4): one inner flow is one sink and one core, about 1.0–1.3 Gbit/s.
- **DPAA RX headroom for an IPv6 outer header**: the seal needs 64 bytes in front of the frame for IPv6 (44 for IPv4); if `f->off` is smaller every IPv6-outer frame is punted. Read `f->off` on the board with C, and raise the headroom if needed.
- **The ~10 s dip** seen in the 140 s `rekey120` run (0.88–0.99 Gbit/s once every ~10 s, before the rekey too): check whether the software path shows it, then correlate with the 2 s aging pass and the 1 s template refresh.
- **Placement by load** (master §4.8 item 5) instead of round-robin.
- **Binding limits**: up to 8 devices; any binding change rebuilds everything (fine for config commits, not for frequent changes).
- **P4 (offline-port reinjection)** shares its unresolved O/H prerequisites with IPsec tier 2. **P5** addresses the requested single-flow multi-core TX scaling and must preserve ordering and replay behavior.
- Generalize `dpaa_wgfp` into the shared steer-to-consumer substrate (master §4.8 item 1) once WireGuard is closed.

### P2b-2 implementation (2026-11-02, gate passed on `.185`)

- `dpaa_wgfp.c` (fsl_dpa): `dpaa_wgfp_flow_add/del/packets()` and `dpaa_wgfp_port_ok()`. They build the F-269 routed key (`0x40|SIP|DIP|..|proto@41|sport@42|dport@44|VID 0|SID 0`, 50 bytes) and call `fman_pcd_fe_flow_add()` with `rx_fqid` = a sink FQID (the same record shape as the ethtool-ntuple steering, F-241) on the ingress port's per-port table. The sink is `hash % sinks`. `dpaa_wgfp_register()` creates the sinks when there are none. There is no `ask.ko` dependency; `ask.ko` already fails closed for flows whose egress is `wg0`, so it never installs a conflicting record.
- `fastpath.c`: `wg_fastpath_learn()`, called from `wg_xmit()` (patch `4018`) for the bound peer. It learns inner IPv4 TCP/UDP flows from a physical DPAA ingress whose conntrack entry is `ESTABLISHED`/`ESTABLISHED_REPLY`, not NAT'ed, without a master and without a helper. It never learns from a SYN. Records are installed by a workqueue, because the record API sleeps. FIN/RST (punted by `tcp_ctl_punt`) withdraws the record, five 2 s aging periods without silicon hits withdraw it, and `wgfp_peer off` withdraws all of them. Failed installs are held for 30 s to avoid retry storms. The `wgfp_peer` read-out adds `flows`, `installs`, `install_fail`, `withdraws` and `decline_withdraws`.
- Seal path fixes: the inner TTL is now decremented (`ip_decrease_ttl`; the lab and P2b-1 forwarded with the TTL unchanged), and TTL ≤ 1 or a dead peer declines. **Any decline of a learned flow withdraws its record** (`wgfp_withdraw_frame()`). Declined frames are dropped by the sink, so an oversized DF flow would otherwise black-hole after install. With the withdraw it falls back to the stack, which handles PMTU. A real punt is still P2b-3.
- Known limits: only flows learned from a packet that still carries a conntrack entry. A flow already in the software flowtable has none; it is tracked from its first established packet, and later packets only count toward admission. A fast-pathed flow skips firewall changes until withdrawn, as an offloaded flowtable flow does. The flow table takes one global spinlock per slow-path packet of the bound peer. The `wgdev` reference held by peer mode blocks deleting `wg0` while bound (P2b-3).
- Harness: `AUTO=1 bin/testrig-wgfp-p2b.sh` skips the debugfs steering and checks that `wgfp_peer off` leaves no records in `fe_flow`.
- **Board result 2026-11-02** (`.185`, image `2026.11.02-0440-rolling`, warm boot after install, tunnel from `KEEP=1 bin/testrig-wireguard.sh setup wg-v4`): `AUTO=1 DUR=20` gave 2.465 Gbit/s up (flows 0.29 / 0.26 / 0.99 / 0.93), 4,491,662 sealed, `install_fail=0`, `decline_withdraws=0`, and `fe_flow` empty after `off`. Install, aging and teardown work. The flow table learns, installs and withdraws on its own. The record's silicon counter (`fe_ehash_stats pkt_count`) tracks `sealed` packet for packet, so aging keeps a busy flow installed for its whole life (0.5 s on-board poll). The 8 installs / 4 withdraws were iperf3's 4 control connections (5 packets each), which got records and aged out after 10 s. **Gate miss cause:** sink choice. The 4 control connections took sinks 0 and 1 (5 frames each), and all 4 data flows hashed onto sinks 2 and 3 (`hash % sinks`), so two cores sealed everything.
- **Fix:** `fastpath.c` now tracks a flow from its first conntrack-proven packet but installs a record only after `WGFP_ADMIT` = 32 slow-path packets. Later packets need no ct, because once the software flowtable has the flow its packets reach `wg_xmit()` without one (seen: conntrack `[OFFLOAD]` for the inner flow). A tracked flow that does not reach the threshold within 10 s is forgotten, so mice never take a record or a sink. Sinks are assigned round-robin per install instead of by tuple hash. This replaces the "learned only at the packet that triggers `flow add`" limit below.
- **Gate PASSED 2026-11-02** on `.185` (image `2026.11.02-1519-rolling`, CI run 41470453447, commit `d072765a`, first boot after install; boot type not recorded): `AUTO=1 DUR=20 bin/testrig-wgfp-p2b.sh` gave 2.942 Gbit/s up (flows 0.64 / 0.66 / 0.65 / 0.99), repeat 3.122 Gbit/s. Exactly 4 installs per run, one per sink (sink frames 1.16M / 1.21M / 1.19M / 1.80M), none for the iperf3 control connections. `install_fail=0`, `decline_withdraws=0`, FIN withdrew every record at flow end, `fe_flow` empty after `off`, MURAM `used` 58764 before and after, no new kernel errors. The remaining imbalance (one flow near 1 Gbit/s, three near 0.6) is per-flow TCP behaviour, not sink placement.

### P3. RX fast path with skb reinjection (gate G3)

The implemented path uses one outer-UDP record per learned peer endpoint and one sink FQ. It copies frames into an skb and hands them to upstream `wg_packet_receive()`, which owns message validation, key selection, decryption, replay and AllowedIPs checks. Decrypted traffic goes through wg0 NAPI/GRO and the normal Linux stack. The original zero-copy, multi-FQ proposal is not implemented; handshake and cookie messages matching the admitted outer tuple can also reach upstream WireGuard through the sink.

**Gate G3:** `down` reaches at least 2.6 Gbit/s on four flows (software today: about 1.58), and the replay-window and out-of-order netns tests pass. **Kill:** below 2.2 Gbit/s. **Effort:** 2 to 3 weeks; it shares the keypair and replay code with P2b.

### P4. Offline-port reinjection (shared with IPsec Tier 2) (gate G4)

Enqueue the authenticated and policy-admitted decrypted frame to the offline port's input FQ so FMan forwards the inner packet. Earlier O/H inject/capture experiments did not deliver a frame to the configured default/error capture sink; whether the input FQ drained is unverified because its count may be byte-swapped. Qdrant records a subsequent patch 0191 KeyGen test that still missed the CC capture sink; that test left the port default/error FQIDs zero. The offline path must be proven independently before integrating it with WireGuard.

**Vendor comparison (2026-11-03, read-only).** The installed `.70` ASK package is `1.2.1~849e1a9112e0`. Its boot log reports a detour on offline port 11, channel `0x80c`, input FQID `0x8057`, and its `detour` module parameter is enabled. The installed `mono-1.2.1` source assigns the detour to `dpa-fman0-oh@4`, which the live boot log maps to `port@85000`. Its `cdx_detour_queue()` uses a `QMAN_FQ_FLAG_TO_DCPORTAL` input FQ with 128 KiB tail drop, FQD context A `OVERRIDE_FQ`, and context B pointing to a separately created exception FQ; it first creates consumers for the configured PCD queues. The vendor sends a received Ethernet frame through two hardware flow records, not through a WireGuard post-decrypt CPU injector. Its software fallback validates the BMan pool, contiguous FD and bounds, and tracks ingress by tuple with duplicate rejection and a grace period.

The historical ASK2 `0175` diagnostic input FQ sets destination and control but not context A/B. It also used a different O/H port and a malformed IPv4 frame, whereas the vendor has configured FMC/PCD and uses received frames. These differences are candidates for a controlled investigation, not proof of the missing-capture cause or grounds to copy FQD settings. Validate management-response byte order and FQ ownership first, then compare the contexts, port configuration and valid-FD prerequisites with the SDK and the live oracle.

The current P3 path copies the received frame into an skb and releases the original BMan buffer before WireGuard decrypts it. The decrypted packet is therefore not automatically available in that original buffer for an in-place offline-port enqueue. A zero-copy design must explicitly transfer or reconstruct buffer ownership across authentication, replay and AllowedIPs checks, DMA mapping, enqueue rejection, and teardown; otherwise use a proven copy-based handoff.

**Gate G4:** injected frames reach the LAN egress with correct L2 rewrite and TTL, with no FE leak and a clean `pcd-snapshot`. **Kill/defer:** if the offline-port FE path wedges or leaks, stop at P3. Decide on P4 jointly with `plans/ASK2-IPSEC-OFFLOAD-PLAN.md`, because both need this substrate. **Effort:** 4 to 8 weeks; this is the highest-risk phase.

### P5. Order restoration for a single inner flow (gate G5)

Do this only if a named workload needs more than about 1.5 Gbit/s per inner flow. The SDK's `qman_enqueue_orp()` uses a 14-bit sequence, ORP FQID and the ORP verb, but the helper alone is not the design: dropped or skipped sequence numbers require a `HOLE` or another proven advance strategy, enqueue rejection can arrive asynchronously, and different input FQs do not automatically share an order-definition stream. Define allocation, sequencing, wraparound, backpressure, teardown and these failure paths before selecting an ORP window; do not assume that a 40-line helper is sufficient.

**Gate G5:** one inner TCP flow reaches at least 2.2 Gbit/s with no extra reorder retransmits. **Kill:** a reject rate above 0.1% or any stall.

Replay, partial-drift rejection, ASan/UBSan and an isolated ARM64 `W=1` QMan object build pass; `qman_priv.h` still emits a packed-alignment warning. No hardware fault was induced.

    This is not a full ownership fix: `qman_destroy_fq()` can still clear the lookup entry after a shutdown error, even though its dynamic FQID remains allocated. The warm-boot/kexec cleanup scans FQID 0, reserved for internal use; whether its query can legitimately return `ERR_FQID` is unverified. If it does, F-306 now fails portal recovery rather than interpreting the error payload as OOS. Validate that behavior before deploying. Same-verb stale results, response overwrites, remote-portal ownership, identity-aware ring dispatch and owner retention remain open. The strict host model still reports two MC and five shutdown counterexamples; the QMan and P3 gates remain blocked.
    **Caller-context constraint (2026-11-07, source audit).** A simple per-portal mutex cannot be added around the MC transaction without first changing its callers: `qman_create_cgr()`, `qman_delete_cgr()`, `qman_update_cgr()`, and `qm_congestion_task()` issue MC commands while holding `cgr_lock` with IRQs disabled; `qm_congestion_task()` explicitly notes that the MC timeout can last 10 ms, too long for a raw spinlock. Separately, `__poll_portal_fast()` documents that user callbacks may call any QMan API, so public MC APIs cannot assume sleepable context. Holding a raw spinlock over the synchronous result poll is also not a valid fix. Any design must first separate callback-list synchronization from command ownership or provide a non-sleeping fail-fast/deferred protocol, then audit how every caller retains objects on busy, timeout, and partial CGR updates. No locking change was attempted.

    **CAAM QI owner audit (2026-11-07, source only).** The prepared kernel enables `CONFIG_CRYPTO_DEV_FSL_CAAM_CRYPTO_API_QI=y`. After `qman_create_fq()` installs an FQ lookup entry, `alloc_rsp_fq_cpu()` frees that FQ if `qman_init_fq()` fails without destroying its lookup entry; `free_rsp_fqs()` also frees previously created response FQs during partial initialization without retiring them. The request path destroys and frees an FQ after an INITFQ error, and its caller unmaps and frees the descriptor context even if the command may complete late. `kill_fq()` frees its FQ after an OOS error, while `caam_drv_ctx_rel()` frees its context even after a failed kill. CAAM also ignores CGR delete errors and may reuse its static CGR object after a failed removal. These are source-derived lifetime hazards, not induced hardware failures. A new MC busy/poison return path cannot be enabled globally until CAAM FQs, descriptor DMA, callbacks, CGRs and controller/devres lifetime are retained together on every failure path.

    **CAAM QI count and DMA corrections (F-301, working tree).** `empty_caam_fq()` masked the raw QMan frame count without converting its big-endian byte order; on little-endian ARM64 a one-frame count could read as zero. The same byte order was verified on `.185` for QMan sink counters, but this CAAM failure was not induced on hardware. F-301 converts the count before masking it and unmaps the request's scatter-gather DMA mapping when `caam_qi_enqueue()` returns a synchronous enqueue error. The source-extracted host model reproduced four counter/failed-enqueue cases before the fix and passes those cases, retries, mapping errors and query errors afterward, including under ASan/UBSan. The corrected CAAM object compiles in an isolated ARM64 build; no image or board test was run. Legacy `qman_enqueue()` still returns success for a full ring, so its silent-loss defect is not fixed and the enqueue-error cases are simulated.

    **CAAM pending-job gate remains open.** `empty_caam_fq()` waits ten times for `refcnt == 1`, then warns and returns success even if an old job is still pending. `bin/test-caam-qi-ownership.py <kernel> --check-pending` reproduces this with a simulated old job and fails. An active external consumer also holds a reference until after its release calls this helper, so the existing aggregate counter does not by itself distinguish that reservation, old jobs and requests queued on the replacement FQ. The update and external-consumer paths publish a new FQ before draining the old one; release also continues after a drain error. Simply returning an error after the wait would expose rollback and concurrent-enqueue lifetime hazards. A safe admission, per-generation completion, rollback and controller-lifetime protocol is still required; correcting the byte order does not prove a drain or unblock the QMan gate.

    **CAAM full-ring containment (F-302, working tree).** The legacy `qman_enqueue()` returns success when the EQCR is full. A source-extracted host model of CAAM with F-301 and the actual QMan enqueue functions reproduced four uncontained full/retry cases across stashed and non-stashed portals. F-302 uses the existing checked `qman_enqueue_fqid()` for CAAM only, retaining the same request FQ as both destination and ERN tag. It holds a context reference continuously across retries rather than briefly returning the aggregate count to idle between attempts. After its existing retry limit it unmaps the request and returns synchronous `-EAGAIN`, rather than the unqueued `-EBUSY` that `crypto_wait_req()` would wait on. The model now passes all four cases, and extracted AEAD and skcipher callers free their descriptors and return immediately on `-EAGAIN`; their submitted-request controls still take the asynchronous path. F-301/F-302 replay and idempotence, host sanitizers and an isolated ARM64 CAAM object compile pass. Other QMan callers still use the unchanged legacy helper; a context reference alone does not solve concurrent FQ replacement, the bounded pending-job drain or parent lifetime. No hardware ring saturation or ERN was induced.

    **CAAM devres registration failure (F-304, working tree).** `devm_add_action_or_reset()` invokes `caam_qi_shutdown()` itself when action registration fails, but the old `caam_qi_init()` then repeated cache, netdev, FQ and CGR cleanup. The extracted-source host model reproduced duplicate releases. F-304 goes directly to freeing the temporary CPU mask after that failure and removes the now-unused duplicate cache-cleanup label; success and registration-failure cases pass normally and under ASan/UBSan. This does not fix errors inside the shutdown action, other probe-unwind paths, or CAAM parent/FQ lifetime.

    **CAAM crypto-parent teardown (2026-11-07, host characterization).** `bin/test-caam-qi-ownership.py <kernel> --check-release` runs the extracted FQ and transform release paths. Four simulated retire, OOS, drain and pending-ORL errors release some FQ or descriptor owner; a successful release control passes. The source also embeds the DMA-mapped key in the crypto transform: even a simulated leaf-only fix retaining the FQ and descriptor still lets `caam_exit_common()` unmap that key and release its job ring, after which `crypto_destroy_tfm()` frees the containing transform. The strict release model therefore reports six uncontained cases, including the actual parent path and the leaf-retention control. The crypto transform's reference count reaches zero before its exit callback; it cannot safely be resurrected there. Safe containment needs an earlier parent reservation or independently owned key storage, plus coordinated FQ, outstanding-request, callback and controller lifetime. This is a source/host result, not a hardware fault.

    **CAAM replacement-FQ rollback after a QMan query error (2026-11-07, source interleaving audit).** `caam_drv_ctx_update()` publishes a new parked request FQ before `empty_caam_fq()` queries the old FQ. On query error it restores the old pointer and calls `kill_fq()` on the replacement. `caam_qi_enqueue()` snapshots `req_fq` with `READ_ONCE()` but has no admission lock shared with the update; an interleaving can therefore submit to the replacement between publication and rollback. `create_caam_req_fq()` sets the ERN callback but leaves `cb.dqrr` unset. If retirement then drains a nonempty replacement with VDQ, QMan's fast DQRR path invokes that callback without a NULL check. This is a source-derived candidate hazard, not a reproduced concurrent operation or silicon fault; the crypto API's setkey/update concurrency contract and the precise reachability of that DQRR path have not been established. The existing CAAM release and pending-job models still report six and one uncontained cases respectively. Do not remove F-305 or add a leaf-only lock/retention workaround: safe handling requires per-generation admission/completion accounting and retention of FQ, descriptor/key, callback and controller owners across unresolved QMan results.

## 6. Risks still open

| Risk | Phase | Containment |
|---|---|---|
| FE wedge or FE-index leak on new record shapes | P2b-2, P3, P4 | Existing record shape; one port at a time; cold boot; panic-on-lockup; `pcd-snapshot` diff |
| Full sink FQ backs up into the FE | P2b-2, P3 | Size the FQ and congestion group before enabling; explicit overload test |
| BMan depletion or double release | P2b to P4 | Release only through `dpaa_fd_release`; 10-minute soak with depletion counters |
| RX INPUT provenance, ingress ambiguity or stale hardware record | P2b-2, P3 | Fail-closed admission and deletion handling; exact-tuple/ingress and endpoint-change tests before production use |
| Full QMan ring or asynchronous TX rejection | P2a to P4 | Local WGFP-only `4019` path; validate retries, ERNs, buffer ownership and teardown on a new image. Legacy callers remain unaudited |
| RX-to-TX DMA direction, translation or bounce-buffer mismatch | P2a to P4 | Audit shared-pool mapping and device domains; do not assume non-coherent or translated DMA is safe |
| Divergence from upstream WireGuard semantics (rekey, roaming, timers, cookies) | P2b-3, P3 | Punt to the stack on any doubt; netns selftest on every change; slow path stays authoritative |
| Memory-bandwidth ceiling near 4 Gbit/s | P2c to P4 | Measure before quoting targets |
| No fast path for a peer behind a non-engaged port; ASK and VPP mutually exclusive per port | product | Documented; the fast path is an add-on for ASK-engaged ports |

## 7. Decision points

1. **Before P2b-2:** resolve the P2a plateau question.
2. **Before P3:** P2b-2 and P2b-3 must pass their gates, because P3 reuses the keypair, replay and registration code.
3. **Before P4:** decide jointly with the IPsec plan whether to build the offline-port reinjection substrate.
4. **Before P5:** go ahead only for a named single-flow workload.
5. **At any kill criterion:** stop that phase, keep everything already shipped and re-plan.
